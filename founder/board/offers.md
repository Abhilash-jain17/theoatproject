# Offers lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Proof is the rare strength.** The buyer of online silver fears fake 925, tarnish and no returns. A Chennai family jeweller with BIS hallmark culture and a physical showroom answers that fear before she asks it, and that trust is already paid for.
- **It asks little time or effort of the buyer.** A wrapped ₹1,999 gift set asks almost nothing of her. Gifting is the sharpest use case here.
- **Buyers with money who are easy to reach.** Women aged 22–38 can be reached through Instagram and WhatsApp, and AJH already has a customer list and AskEva. Acquisition starts warm.
- **Pricing discipline.** No "up to" offers and no discount language keep it out of a race to the bottom.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity with a nice wrapper.** "925 silver at ₹1,999/₹2,499" is a product and a price, not an offer. The pain is a want, not a massive pain, and buyers compare online silver on price in seconds. How crowded the market is remains unknown (/founder-competitors).
2. **No guarantee and no real urgency.** There is no promise on tarnish, buyback or exchange. Launch-week free shipping becomes an expectation, not scarcity.
3. **Margin is unknown.** Unit cost, returns and CAC are not known. If margin is thin, a guarantee, premium wrap and free shipping together could wipe out the profit on a ₹1,999 order (/founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is left from a ₹1,999 sale after landed cost, shipping, wrap and returns? (/founder-cfo)
- Will AJH stand behind silver the way it stands behind gold, with exchange value, lifetime cleaning and tarnish replacement?
- Who is the first buyer, the self-purchaser or the gifter? What problems does she hit on the way to buying? (/founder-consumer)
- Could you charge ₹2,999 for the gift set and still be the obvious choice?
- What do other ₹1,500–3,000 silver sellers offer that you do not? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Build a named stack: a hallmark and authenticity certificate, a guarantee the CFO has costed (an exchange credit or tarnish replacement), free lifetime polish at the showroom, and a gift-ready delivery-date promise.
2. Replace launch-week free shipping with real scarcity: small numbered drops, shown to the WhatsApp list first and never restocked.
3. Fund stock only once the CFO's unit economics show a healthy margin per order with the stack included.
4. Run a consumer panel on the stacked offer against the bare product. Proceed only if the stack clearly lifts the buy rate.
