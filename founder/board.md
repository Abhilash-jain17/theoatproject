## The risks more than one member raised (most dangerous first)
1. **A commodity product with a nice wrapper (all three).** "925 silver at ₹1,999/₹2,499" is not yet a product, an offer or a position. AJH's trust is the only difference named so far. The Offers lens calls it a product and a price, not an offer. The Monopoly lens says there is no secret and no barrier. The Product lens says there is no "one thing" and no design signature.
2. **Unit margin is unknown, and so is the CAC it can carry (all three).** Free shipping, premium wrap, a guarantee and returns could eat the profit on a ₹1,999 order before ads are even counted.
3. **Trust may not travel (Monopoly, Product).** The AJH name carries weight in Chennai and in a showroom. Outside South India, and through a phone screen, it has to be rebuilt from photos, packaging and delivery. Going pan-India early burns the budget. A cheap-feeling first delivery damages the parent brand.

## Where the board disagrees
- **How much does trust count?** The Offers lens treats AJH's trust as the rare strength that makes the offer work. The Monopoly lens treats it as a head start that does not protect the business, because brand alone will not stop copycats. The Product lens says trust is only real if the online experience delivers it.
- **What to fix first.** Offers: the stack and the guarantee. Monopoly: the market to own and the secret. Product: the design signature and the online experience. All three apply; the order below follows the pack.

- [ ] **Online experience tested before paid spend:** unboxing, card, care, sizing and returns tried on real showroom customers. → `/founder-ops`, `/founder-launch`

## The strongest version the board can see
This is not "a pan-India online silver brand"; that version is a commodity listing buying expensive ads. The strongest version is **the silver on-ramp to a family jeweller.** It is a tight, signature-designed drop range sold first to Chennai/South India women who already know AJH, or whose mothers do. Every piece carries proof and promises a pure-online seller cannot match: a hallmark certificate, free lifetime polish at the showroom, and the right to exchange silver toward a first gold piece. That last promise is the moat. It turns a ₹1,999 gift into the start of a 15-year relationship with AJH: first silver, then lightweight gold, then the bridal ticket. Measured that way, Silver Story's real return is customer lifetime value to AJH, not silver margin. The CFO should model it on that basis. That is a different business from the one pitched: smaller and local at first, but defensible.
